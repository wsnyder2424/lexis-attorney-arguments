Motion Brief
Defendant's Motion to Dismiss for Failure to State a Claim Under 35 U.S.C. § 101
TidalWave Networks, LLC v. Vellum Semiconductor, Inc.  |  S.D. Cal.  |  Filed Mar. 4, 2024  |  3:23-cv-02841-BAS-DEB
Download | Email | Print | Share
I. Introduction

Plaintiff's complaint asserts infringement of U.S. Patent No. 10,847,482 ("the '482 patent"). The asserted claims, on their face, are directed to nothing more than the long-standing practice of collecting, analyzing, and displaying network performance data. Reciting that practice on a generic computer does not make it patent-eligible. The complaint should be dismissed under Rule 12(b)(6) because the asserted claims are ineligible for patent protection under 35 U.S.C. § 101.

II. The Asserted Patent

The '482 patent is titled "System and Method for Monitoring Network Performance." Independent Claim 1 recites a method comprising: (a) receiving network performance data from one or more endpoints; (b) aggregating the received data; (c) comparing the aggregated data against a stored performance baseline; and (d) displaying the comparison result on a user interface. The remaining asserted claims add only generic computing components, such as a processor, memory, and a network interface.

III. Argument
A. The Two-Step Alice Framework Governs This Motion

The Supreme Court's decision in Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank International sets out the framework for evaluating patent eligibility under § 101. At step one, the court determines whether the claims are directed to a patent-ineligible concept such as an abstract idea. If they are, at step two, the court asks whether the claim elements, individually or as an ordered combination, recite an "inventive concept" sufficient to transform the abstract idea into a patent-eligible application.

B. The Asserted Claims Are Directed to an Abstract Idea
Surfaced argument

The asserted claims of the '482 patent are directed to the abstract idea of collecting, analyzing, and displaying network performance data, and they recite nothing more than generic computer components performing routine functions. Under the two-step framework of Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank International, the claims are ineligible for patent protection under 35 U.S.C. § 101. The Federal Circuit has repeatedly held that claims directed to gathering data, applying a comparison, and presenting the result fall squarely within the abstract-idea category, and the '482 claims add no technical improvement that would distinguish them from that line of authority.

C. The Claims Recite No Inventive Concept at Step Two

At step two of the Alice inquiry, the asserted claims recite only well-understood, routine, and conventional computer components performing well-understood, routine, and conventional functions. Receiving data over a network, storing it in memory, comparing it against a baseline value, and rendering the result on a display were each standard computing operations long before the priority date of the '482 patent. Reciting these operations together, in the order one would naturally perform them, does not supply the inventive concept that § 101 requires.

D. The Claims Are Properly Resolved at the Pleading Stage

Resolution of § 101 eligibility at the pleading stage is appropriate where, as here, the patent's intrinsic record forecloses any plausible inventive-concept theory. The Federal Circuit has affirmed Rule 12(b)(6) dismissals in materially similar circumstances, and judicial economy favors resolving eligibility before the parties incur the substantial expense of claim construction and discovery.

IV. Conclusion

For the foregoing reasons, Defendant respectfully requests that the Court dismiss Plaintiff's complaint in its entirety, with prejudice, under 35 U.S.C. § 101.
